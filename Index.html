What is an Aqueduct?

Aqueducts are structures built to carry water, often for irrigation. They were used in the ancient world before the invention of modern plumming. An aqueduct can be as primitive as a simple ditch dug into the ground, or as complex as the large brick structures that the Romans were famous for. The Romans made aqueducts so common that they became known as the "arteries of Europe"
